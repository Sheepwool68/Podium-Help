# Weather and batteries

**The two things that end a race day are a flat battery and water in the wrong place.** Neither
announces itself, and both are entirely preventable the night before.

## Water is the enemy of a read

A UHF tag has no battery. The reader has to power it and then hear a very quiet answer back, and
**water absorbs exactly the signal doing both jobs.**

So rain does not break the equipment. It quietly costs you reads:

- **A soaked shirt over a bib tag** is a tag with water between it and the antenna.
- **Standing water on the line**, or saturated ground, changes what the antenna is working against.
- **A wet body** screens a tag better than a dry one, so a bunch finish in the rain is harder than
  the same finish in the dry.

**Test in the conditions you will run in.** A dry-morning test proves the dry morning. If the
forecast is wet, the useful test is a wet one — and if you cannot get one, plan the backup instead
([back-everything-up.md](back-everything-up.md)).

An active tag carries its own battery and announces itself, so it forgives weather far better. If
you have a choice of tag for a wet event, that is the consideration.

## Keeping the kit alive

- **The box goes out of the weather.** It is the thing that must survive the day. Under cover, off
  the ground, out of standing water.
- **Cables are where water gets in**, and a connector sitting in a puddle is the one that fails at
  hour four. Bring them in from above where you can, and tape them down where people walk
  ([where-to-put-the-mat.md](where-to-put-the-mat.md)).
- **Condensation is real.** Kit brought from a cold vehicle into a warm marquee, or the reverse,
  gets wet on the inside. Give it time.

## Batteries

**Cold flattens a battery faster than the same day would warm.** A winter cross-country or a long
night section takes more out of everything than the number on the spec sheet suggests.

Charge everything the night before, including the ones nobody thinks of:

- **The boxes.**
- **The phone or laptop doing the timing.** Holding radio links to boxes all day is hard work for a
  phone, and it is the device you cannot do without.
- **The active tags.** They are battery powered. Tags that have been in a crate since last season
  get tested, not assumed.

**Watch the battery column rather than remembering to think about it.** Both apps show each box's
battery, and a box that is going flat tells you so long before it goes quiet. A box that has already
gone quiet looks identical to a box out of range or switched off
([box-will-not-connect.md](box-will-not-connect.md)).

## Long events

For a twelve or twenty-four hour race, treat power as part of the plan rather than a check:

1. **Know the runtime**, and know it for the conditions — a cold night is not a mild afternoon.
2. **Have the changeover worked out before you need it**, including who does it and what happens to
   the reads while it happens. The box keeps its own log, so a box that is off the link is not
   losing anything ([recover-missed-reads.md](recover-missed-reads.md)).
3. **Check in through the event**, not at the end. See [lap-racing.md](lap-racing.md).

## If a box goes quiet anyway

**The reads are not lost.** Every box keeps its own log, so a box that timed a whole race flat on
its link still has everything on it. Deal with the link, then pull the reads off
([reads-stopped-during-a-race.md](reads-stopped-during-a-race.md)).

What you cannot recover is a read the box never took. That is why the backup matters and why the
tag placement matters — a flat box is fixable, a tag nobody could read is not.
