# Where the chip goes on a competitor

**A chip that cannot be seen by the antenna cannot be read, however good the mat is.** More lost
times come from where the tag was worn than from anything wrong with the equipment.

## The rule behind all of it

**The tag wants to be on the outside of the person, clear of water and metal, and facing the way it
will pass the antenna.**

A UHF tag has no battery. The reader powers it and then listens for a very quiet answer, so a body,
a soaked shirt or a bike frame in the way costs you both halves of that exchange. An active tag
carries its own battery and announces itself, so it forgives far more — but a tag buried under three
layers and a race cape is asking for trouble either way.

## Common placements, and what each one costs

- **Ankle** — reliable, because it is low, clear of the body and close to a mat on the ground. It
  wants to be on the outside of the ankle, snug enough not to spin round and not so tight it marks.
- **On the bib** — convenient, because the number and the chip arrive together and nobody has to
  hand anything back. It depends on the bib staying on the **front** and staying flat. A bib folded
  up under a jacket, or turned to the back, is a tag that has gone quiet.
- **On the bike** — fork, frame or number plate, depending on the tag. Keep it off the frame's metal
  where you can and make sure it is not shrouded by a bottle, a bag or the rider's leg.
- **Swim** — wrist or ankle under the wetsuit sleeve is normal, but water is the hardest thing UHF
  has to fight. Talk to RFID Timing about the right tag before running a swim on tags you have not
  used in water.

## Tell the competitors, in one sentence

Most lost times are self-inflicted at registration. A line on the entry confirmation and a sign at
the desk does more good than anything you can do at the mat:

> Wear the tag on the outside, on the front, and do not fold, cut or cover it.

**Do not let anybody run with the tag in a pocket, under a bag strap, or pinned to the back**, and
do not let them cut a bib down to fit — the tag is usually part of what gets cut off.

## Handing them out

**A chip has to be against a person in Podium before they start**, not just in their hand. The
handing out and the recording are two jobs and it is the second one that produces results. See
[where-chip-codes-come-from.md](where-chip-codes-come-from.md).

- **Hand out in number order** where the tags are encoded in number order. Then the rule does the
  recording for you and there is nothing to type.
- **Every swap gets recorded at the moment it happens.** A tag swapped at the line and written on a
  hand is a tag that scores the wrong person. Do it there and then, one at a time on the entrant.
- **Check the "Not ready" list before the gun.** It is the list of people who will get no result,
  and no chip is the main way onto it.

## Getting them back

**Decide before the event whether you are collecting tags, and tell people at the finish.** A
collection funnel has to be part of the finish layout, not an afterthought once the winner is
through — see [running-a-finish.md](running-a-finish.md).

Tags that walk home are next season's missing tags. If your club reissues the same tag to the same
racer each time, the carry-over route depends on getting them back
([entrants-and-numbers.md](entrants-and-numbers.md)).
