# Running a finish

**The finish is the one line you cannot redo.** A split can be reconstructed from a finish and a
pace. A missing finish is somebody's whole race.

## A finish is the first read, and that is deliberate

A mat reads a chip several times as somebody runs across it, and then keeps reading them while they
stand behind the line getting their breath back. **The moment they crossed is the first of those
reads**, so that is the one Podium takes.

The gate is what stops the standing-about reads becoming a second finish. See
[timing-points.md](timing-points.md).

## Give them somewhere to go

**The single most useful thing at a finish is a funnel that keeps moving.** It is not about tidiness:

- People who stop on the mat sit in the read zone, which is work the system has to undo.
- People who wander back across the line can collect a read that looks like a second crossing.
- A bunch that bunches gets screened — bodies between tag and antenna is the main way a read is
  lost ([where-to-put-the-mat.md](where-to-put-the-mat.md)).

Walk them through, out the far side, and only then collect tags, hand out water, or take names.

## Bunch and sprint finishes

**Two people crossing together is not a problem for the mat — it is a problem for the read.** The
timing is fine to the millisecond; what fails is one tag being screened by the other body.

Three things help, in order of how much they help:

1. **A wide enough line** that shoulder-to-shoulder arrivals are not stacked front to back.
2. **Tags worn on the outside and on the front**
   ([where-the-chip-goes.md](where-the-chip-goes.md)).
3. **Somebody writing the order down**, which is the only thing that saves you when the first two
   were not enough ([back-everything-up.md](back-everything-up.md)).

**Do not settle a close finish from the chip times alone if a placing is in dispute.** Go back to
the reads themselves — [raw-data.md](raw-data.md).

## Watch the results as they come, not at the end

**A mat that has stopped reading looks exactly like a course with nobody on it.** The difference is
that you can still fix the first one while the race is running.

So keep the results screen in front of somebody, and treat a gap as a fault until proven otherwise:

- **Nobody at all for a while** — check the mat is still delivering
  ([box-connected-but-no-reads.md](box-connected-but-no-reads.md)).
- **Several people from the same wave missing** — the mat, not the runners
  ([runner-shows-no-time.md](runner-shows-no-time.md)).
- **One person missing** — usually the tag, and usually how it was worn.

## Collecting tags

If you are taking tags back, **the collection point goes after the funnel, not on the line.** People
stopping to unfasten an ankle strap in the read zone is the thing you were trying to avoid.

## Before you take the line down

1. **Is everybody accounted for?** Anybody still out on course, and anybody marked as not finished
   who actually did.
2. **Has the backlog reached zero?** That is the count of reads still to reach the server. Packing
   up mid-count is how a race arrives incomplete.
3. **Only then** unplug anything.
